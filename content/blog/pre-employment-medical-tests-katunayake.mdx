If you live or work around **[Katunayake](/locations/katunayake)** — the airport, the **Free Trade Zone (Export Processing Zone)**, or one of the many factories and airline offices nearby — there's a good chance you'll be asked for a **medical test** at some point. It might be a **pre-employment check** before you start a factory or airport job, or a **visa / overseas-job medical** before you travel for work abroad.

This guide explains, in plain language, what those tests usually involve, what each one costs in LKR, and how to get the blood-test parts done **without taking a day off to queue at a lab**.

## Pre-employment vs. visa medicals — they're not the same

It helps to know which one you actually need, because the tests differ.

| | **Pre-employment (local job)** | **Visa / overseas-job medical** |
|---|---|---|
| Who asks for it | A factory, the FTZ, airport or airline employer | The embassy or a licensed agency (e.g. for Gulf jobs) |
| Purpose | Confirm you're fit for the role | Meet the destination country's entry health rules |
| Typical parts | Blood tests + a basic physical | Blood tests **+ chest X-ray + physical** at a designated centre |
| Where blood is drawn | Can be done at home or on-site | Blood parts can be done early; some centres require their own draw |

**Important and honest:** a full **GAMCA / official visa medical** (for many Gulf countries) has to be completed at a *government-approved medical centre* that also does the chest X-ray and doctor's examination. We are a **diagnostic laboratory**, so we handle the **blood-test components** and **pre-employment screening** — we don't issue the official visa medical certificate. Always confirm with your **agency or employer** exactly which tests and which centre they require before you book anything.

## The blood tests most commonly requested

Here are the tests that come up again and again on pre-employment and pre-departure forms, what each one actually checks, and the price at St. Luke's.

- **Full Blood Count (FBC)** — LKR 400. A general health snapshot: anaemia, infection, and blood-cell levels. On almost every form.
- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)** — LKR 280. Screens for diabetes. Needs 8–12 hours fasting, so best done first thing in the morning.
- **HbA1c** — LKR 2,100. A 3-month average blood-sugar picture; some employers ask for this instead of, or with, FBS.
- **Blood Group** — LKR 550. Often required so your employer has it on record.
- **Serum Creatinine** — LKR 650. A basic kidney-function check.
- **Lipid Profile** — LKR 1,500. Cholesterol and related fats; sometimes part of a fuller fitness check.
- **HIV 1 & 2** — LKR 1,800. Commonly required for overseas-job medicals.
- **VDRL** — LKR 1,000. Screens for syphilis; another standard overseas-job item.
- **Malarial Parasites** — LKR 250. Sometimes requested depending on the destination.
- **TSH (thyroid)** — LKR 1,800. Occasionally asked for.
- **Pregnancy Test (HCG)** — LKR 250. Required for some female applicants before travel.

Your form may list a slightly different combination — send us the list and we'll tell you exactly which we can do and the total.

## Prepare properly so you don't have to repeat a test

- **Fasting tests (FBS, Lipid Profile):** nothing but water for **8–12 hours** beforehand. Morning is easiest.
- **Bring your NIC / passport details** — names on the report must match your job or visa paperwork exactly.
- **Double-check the list** with your agency first. Redoing a missed test costs time you may not have before a departure date.
